Why Controller-9

No program to fight with. No ads.

We read 454 public reviews of phone-controller apps. The same complaints come up everywhere: a computer program that won't connect, ads in the middle of a game, and subscriptions for basic buttons. Controller-9 is built the other way.

Android

Nothing to install.

Your phone pairs as an Xbox Wireless Controller from the computer's own Bluetooth settings. Tested with a game on a Mac; Linux's controller layer (SDL) sees it too.

Always

Free means free.

No ads, no subscription, no account, no analytics. Every feature for everyone.

New

It tells you why.

When the computer can't be reached, the connection check names the cause — app closed, firewall, VPN, a different Wi-Fi — and the fix.

New

Move any button.

Customise any layout: drag and resize each button on its own, from Xbox to arcade stick.

Tested

Every press, exactly.

An automated test presses every control: 20 of 20 arrive as themselves, and two buttons held at once stay held together.

Mac

Not Windows-only.

Mac games see the Android phone through Apple's own controller support, and Safari and Chrome see a standard gamepad.

How it works

Three steps. About a minute.

On Android there is nothing to install on the computer. iPhone uses a small free companion app, because iOS doesn't let phones act as Bluetooth controllers.

01

Pair once

Android: turn on Bluetooth controller, then choose “Xbox Wireless Controller” in the computer's Bluetooth settings. iPhone: scan the companion's code.

02

Pick a layout

Seven familiar controller faces, each lit in your game's colour.

03

Play

It reconnects on its own next time. If it can't, the app tells you why and what to do.

Touch

One press, one control.

Verified on an iPhone 15: twenty controls pressed one by one, each sent only itself. A thumb resting between buttons presses nothing.

Rumble

Games can shake your hands.

Two-motor rumble from the game, rendered on the phone's own haptics — heavy hits pulse, light ones hum.

Links

Two links, one session.

Wi-Fi and Bluetooth carry the same input side by side. Lose one and the other keeps you playing.

New · Android

No app on the computer.

Your phone pairs as an Xbox Wireless Controller from the Mac's own Bluetooth settings. Tested: Asphalt Legends on a Mac shows Xbox prompts and is played from the phone.

New · Android

Locked in the game.

Controller Lock pins the pad: no notification panel, home or back swipe mid-race. Hold to unlock. Notifications stay quiet until you leave.

New · Android

Black glass, your colour.

Smoked-glass controls that light in each game's tone — platinum, champagne, crimson, sapphire, emerald, copper. Turbo on any button.

New · Android

Aim by turning.

Gyro aim moves the right stick as you turn the phone, like a DualSense. Tested by hand: full stick travel left, right, up and down.

New · Android

Cloud gaming, in the browser.

Safari and Chrome on a Mac read the phone as a standard gamepad — what browser cloud-gaming services use.

New · Android

Share your setup.

A game's layout, colour, steering and turbo buttons travel phone to phone as a QR code. Friends join from the Home screen.

New · Android

One tap from anywhere.

A Controller tile in Quick Settings turns the Bluetooth controller on or off without opening the app.

New · Android

Straight into the game.

Long-press the app icon: "Play Asphalt Legends" opens that game's controller, lit in its colour.

New · Android

GameCube and arcade.

A big-A GameCube-style pad with C-stick, and an arcade stick in the standard fight-stick button order.
